Four questions worth answering

Everything else I can assume safely. These four genuinely change the size of the project.

What sits behind Client Login?

Client Login appears in the navigation, the forms table and the user journeys — but no account screens exist anywhere, in the brief or the file. Right now it is a door into an empty room. This is the biggest variable in the estimate.

How does money actually change hands?

Your file says the cart flow is for services that need approval first. Worth questioning: after you approve someone three days later, asking them to come back and re-add the service to a cart is friction with no payoff. Most platforms also will not let you redesign their checkout at all.

How many service pages are genuinely different?

The file has two so far — Brand Strategy and Email Marketing — and they share a structure. One good template costs a fraction of bespoke pages and keeps the site consistent as you add services.

Does Work link to anything?

Work sits in the navigation and in the homepage sections, but the brief stops there — no case pages exist in the structure or in the Figma file. Same shape of gap as Client Login. For a studio selling strategy, the case study is the sales argument: a grid of pretty thumbnails proves you can art direct, a written case proves you can think.

Dials you can turn

The defaults are what I would recommend. Change any of them and the panel updates.

Screen sizes

Your visitors are creative directors and founders on large monitors — 1920 and wider is where your work gets judged, and where editorial layouts fail most quietly. Without a decision about where the page stops growing, a hero designed at 1440 becomes a thin strip of text in empty space.

Rounds of revisions

A round is one consolidated pass of feedback across a whole stage, not a stream of separate comments.

What I have assumed

Correct anything that is wrong and I will adjust. Silence means these hold.

Assumptions

  • Notes, positioning and any existing photos come from you — rough is fine. I write the copy and generate the imagery from there.
  • Feedback arrives consolidated, one voice per round.
  • Accessible by default — readable contrast, visible focus, keyboard-reachable forms, motion that respects reduced-motion settings. No extra charge, no formal audit either.
  • Delivery is Figma — organised, named, developer-ready.
  • Timeline assumes roughly 32 focused hours a week.
  • Legal pages (privacy, terms, refunds) get one simple text template; the wording comes from your side.

Not included

  • Development — I hand over designs and support the build. If you have not lined up a developer yet, say so: I build sites too and can quote that separately.
  • Photo shoots with a photographer, model or location.
  • Font licences — bought in your name.
  • SEO strategy and analytics setup.
  • Brand identity work — your guide is complete; I work inside it.
  • Ongoing maintenance after handoff, unless we agree a retainer.

Stages and payment

Each stage ends with something you approve before the next begins, so the project cannot quietly drift away from what you expected.
